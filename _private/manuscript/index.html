A speculative landing page for an imagined product

A place to write
replicable academic papers.

Manuscript is an editor for research writing. You develop the ideas and shape the argument; a quiet assistant handles the formatting, the cleaning, the cross-checks, and the citations — the work that used to stand between a finished thought and a finished paper.

Tour the concept → See the editor 5 screens · ~3 min walkthrough
This is fiction. No product exists.
01The idea

The researcher does the thinking. The software does the rest.

Writing a paper is mostly not writing. It's formatting tables, chasing citations, re-running a figure, checking a number against a spreadsheet, threading revisions. Manuscript is built to do that work quietly in the background — so the hours you spend at your desk are the hours that actually move the argument forward.

Principle 01

Your ideas. Your voice.

The assistant doesn't draft your argument — you do. It handles the paperwork around the argument: formatting, consistency, reference plumbing, figure regeneration. The sentences that matter stay yours.

you write · it tidies
Principle 02

Replicable by default.

Every figure traces back to its data; every statistic to the cell it was computed from; every claim to the paper it came from. The work checks itself as you write, so reviewers (and your future self) find fewer mistakes.

draft ⇄ data ⇄ sources
Principle 03

From desk to DOI, faster.

The weeks that usually disappear into formatting, re-typesetting for each journal, and reconciling revisions — compressed into the click that says "ready." More time at the whiteboard; less time at the styleguide.

ideas in · papers out
02Five surfaces

What would this look like, day to day?

Five surfaces a researcher actually uses — the draft, the data, the figures, the instruments, the prior literature. Each one designed so the person does the thinking and the software handles the rest.

Screen 01·Manuscript ↗

The manuscript is the app.

Draft, publication, and interactive reading modes — from one file. You write in the margin; the typeset page rebuilds itself. No re-formatting between journals, no copy-paste, no last-minute LaTeX.
02·Data↗

Raw and Clean,
side by side.

A workspace for the tables and transcripts you're writing about. Private by default; shareable when you say so.
03·Figures↗

Figures that stay editable.

Nudge a palette, toggle error bars, drop the figure into the draft at a named heading. No round-trip through Illustrator.
04·Materials↗

Every instrument,
in one place.

Surveys, protocols, stimuli, consent forms, notes. Versioned alongside the draft so you always know what went with what.
05·Library↗

A library that remembers what you read.

The papers you've read, organized by the ideas you read them for. Every highlight traces back to the sentence in your draft that needed it.
