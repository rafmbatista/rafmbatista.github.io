Welcome to Manuscript.

An imagined product. Inspired by and mocked up by Claude Design for academic research.

If Manuscript existed, I could open it and find every part of a study in one place. The instruments I built — surveys, protocols, consent forms. The papers I read in the run-up. The data those instruments collected. The figures I drew from it. And at the center of it all, the paper itself.

What follows is a walk through what that might look like. None of it works.

01 — Materials

Where a study begins.

Every empirical paper stands on its instruments–––the survey, the interview protocol, the consent form, the pre-analysis plan. Most of us today save these in folders on our computer, if they are even saved at all. Some of us (or most of us at least some of the time), will download the survey from Qualtrics or the pre-registration from AsPredicted each and every time. In Manuscript they would sit together, versioned, so nothing is forgotten or overlooked.

For example, the pre-registration is a commit, not a PDF. Every deviation from the plan is a row in an audit log that is visible, named, and quietly drafted into the limitations paragraph for me to tighten later.

02 — Library

Knowledge is connected.

Manuscript could ingest a reading list, files, photos, or entire books, stored in a project library. It would borrow tools we see today in Google's NotebookLM or Obsidian, where your library is easily turned to a graph, where papers cluster by theme. For the papers you've read, you can collect excerpts. For those you haven't but cite because reviewers asked you to, the relevant sections are highlighted for you. Citations now serve as a nudge, when you include a source you'll be prompted to tie it back to an excerpt. No hallucinations---from the machine or the human---when you have to cite your sources this way.

03 — Data

Raw data is protected. Cleaned data is made accessible.

Raw and Clean live side by side. Raw is encrypted, PII-flagged, never leaves the device. Clean is anonymized, pipeline-traced so viewers can see how it was cleaned, ready to deposit at OSF. Manuscript would handle all types of data, helping you parse qualitative and quantitative insights alike. It could check for errors automatically and summarize descriptives easily.

04 — Figures

Figures are tuned instantly and visually.

In Manuscript a figure is a live R script bound to clean data. Like Claude Design's "Tweaks" in Manuscript you could easily Tweak a palette, toggle error bars, swap the type of figure you want at the click of a button — the figure redraws, the script regenerates, and the new version is easily saved into your manuscript. When the data refreshes, the figure refreshes too. No more re-export, re-paste, and hope it's the right version.

05 — Paper

And finally, the paper renders for any audience.

One file, three views. Draft is the work in progress with links back to specific analyses, datasets, and highlighted excerpts. Publication is what the journal sees — typeset and polished. Interactive renders what a reader sees on the web, where the numbers are live and they can filter the data themselves. The text doesn't change between views; the template does. Switching journals is switching a template, not re-formatting.

None of this exists.

But it could. The Library draws on features that can be found in Google's NotebookLM, Obsidian, and DEVONThink4. Claude Code and OpenAI's Codex have made cleaning, wrangling, analyzing, and visualizing data efficient and accessible. What's more, DEVONThink has shown us this can live on our personal machines, Manuscript would then serve as a wrapper, organizing the system most of us already use. Claude Design showed us that a system like this is not in the distant future, it both served as inspiration and assistant in mocking up the frontend. 

For an afternoon's worth of work, it was fun to imagine what the future holds for research. I think a tool like this would be disruptive for academia but not for academics. Academia relies on publications to judge new hires and decide who to promote, journals in turn profit immensely from a system of publish-or-perish. Academics dedicate countless hours to the publication process that goes far beyond the invention of new ideas and the actual research. I believe a tool like this, should its designers will it, could free up time for thinking.
